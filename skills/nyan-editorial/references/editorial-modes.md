# Optional editorial modes

These are local adaptations. Use only the mode the work needs.

## Interview or brainstorm

Ask the next unanswered question that will change the draft, one at a time:

- What happened, or what exact situation are we writing for?
- What do you think about it, in your own words?
- Who needs this, and what should they understand, feel, or do?
- Which example, observation, or source supports that?
- What feels wrong about the obvious way of saying it?
- What would change your view?

Offer short contrasting phrasings when reactions are easier than explanation. Label new angles as proposals. Never manufacture a personal anecdote to supply stakes. Reflect back when useful rather than after a fixed number of answers.

A compact idea check can name the central point, reader payoff, best material, and one unresolved gap. Recommend pursue, reshape, or park only when useful. Do not flatter the author or line-edit an undeveloped idea.

## Substantive editor report

For full draft review, use as much of this shape as helps:

1. Strongest specific idea and whether readers encounter it early enough.
2. Critical fixes to claim, support, structure, and reader promise.
3. Short map of sentence-level corrections in document order.
4. The next revision with the greatest effect.

Material flags need a location and actionable fix. Evidence gaps may need a question, source, narrower statement, or cut; do not supply an apparently exact rewrite containing invented evidence. Separate necessary fixes from taste. Do not claim exhaustive error detection.

## Structural AI residue check

Examine unsupported details and changed certainty before vocabulary. Look for overly neat causality, artificial agreement between every example and the thesis, generic emotional uplift, symmetrical conclusions, and sentences congratulating or explaining the argument instead of making it.

- **Deletion test:** does removing the line lose information or only polish?
- **Referent test:** can the reader identify the thing behind an abstraction or vague pronoun?

A metaphor should explain a real relationship. Preserve specific domain language, meaningful repetition, corrective words, and uncertainty that matches evidence. Do not insert mistakes or quirks to simulate humanity.

## Social adaptation

Build from supported material and the intended reader response. Separate quotations from paraphrased hooks. Vary the angle, not just the opening words. A teaser can convey feeling without mechanics; a launch can pair feeling with an accurate offer and action. Reminder urgency must follow real dates or availability, never invented scarcity.

Use the project's approved voice and cultural context. Do not copy Every's voice into SDB or SpringBees. Hashtags, emojis, variant counts, and formats follow the brief.
